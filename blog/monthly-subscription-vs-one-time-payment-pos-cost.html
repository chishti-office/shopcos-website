Monthly Subscription vs One-Time Payment: What a POS Really Costs Over 3 Years

November 9, 202611 min readPricing Guide

"Rs 1,500 a month" sounds like nothing. "Rs 40,000 once" sounds like a lot. That is exactly why so many shopkeepers in Pakistan pick the monthly plan without doing the maths — and why, three years later, some of them have paid twice the price of the one-time licence and still own nothing.

The question is not which number is smaller on day one. The question is: what do you pay over the life of your shop? A POS system is not a one-month purchase. If your shop is still open in three years — and you bought software to run it — you will still be paying for it. So the honest comparison is three years of monthly payments against one payment, once.

This guide does that maths with real Pakistani prices, shows where each model hides its costs, and ends with the seven questions that settle the choice for your shop. No vendor spin — including ours. The numbers are the numbers.

Why the Monthly Price Feels Cheaper

It feels cheaper because the number is small, and because a monthly fee feels like a utility bill — electricity, mobile load, shop rent. You pay it, you forget it, life goes on. A one-time payment feels like buying furniture: a "big" decision that needs thinking about, permission from the family, and a brave moment at the counter.

Vendors know this. That is why the monthly figure is printed big on the pricing page and the yearly total is printed small — or not at all. A plan advertised as "only Rs 2,000/month" is really a Rs 24,000-a-year commitment, and a Rs 72,000 commitment over three years. Nobody puts that second number in the headline.

But a shop is not a streaming service. You do not cancel your billing software when the month is slow — the counter still needs it every single day, in the rush and in the quiet hours. The right comparison is never one month against one payment. It is the total cost of ownership over the years you will actually use it.

Monthly POS plans advertised in Pakistan in late 2026 ranged from about Rs 500 to Rs 6,999 a month, with most mid-range plans sitting between Rs 1,500 and Rs 3,000. Against a one-time licence of Rs 40,000 — the shopcos soft lifetime price — here is what each monthly fee actually costs:

That is a second computer, a barcode printer, a year's worth of thermal rolls, and still change left over. The "cheap" monthly plan bought all of that — and then gave it away.

The Break-Even Point: When One-Time Starts Winning

The break-even point is simple: divide Rs 40,000 by the monthly fee. That is the month the one-time licence becomes the cheaper choice — and every month after it is pure saving.

  • Rs 500/month: break-even at about 80 months — over six and a half years. On pure price, monthly wins here. (Read the catch in the next section but one.)
  • Rs 1,500/month: break-even at about 27 months — just over two years.
  • Rs 3,000/month: break-even at about 13 months — just over one year.
  • Rs 5,000/month: break-even at 8 months.
  • Rs 6,999/month: break-even at under 6 months.

Here is the rule of thumb to carry to the shop: if the monthly fee is above roughly Rs 1,100, a Rs 40,000 one-time licence is cheaper within three years (Rs 40,000 ÷ 36 months = Rs 1,111). And after break-even, the subscription keeps charging every month for something you could have owned outright. At Rs 3,000 a month, year four and year five cost another Rs 72,000 — while the one-time licence costs zero. Stretch the table to five years and the gap at Rs 3,000 a month becomes Rs 140,000.

The 5 Hidden Costs of Monthly Subscriptions

The table above assumes the monthly price never changes and covers everything you need. In practice, it usually does neither. These are the five costs that never appear in the headline price:

1. Price rises

The Rs 1,500 you signed up at is rarely the Rs 1,500 you pay in year two. Vendors raise prices — a "revised plan", a "new pricing structure" — and the renewal notice arrives quietly, after the money has already left your account. Before you sign up, ask whether the price is locked, for how long, and get the answer in writing. A price that can rise 20% a year turns your three-year table into fiction.

2. Per-user and per-branch fees

The headline price often covers one counter and one user. Add a second counter, a second cashier login, or a second branch, and the fee doubles — or triples. The "Rs 2,000 a month" plan becomes Rs 4,000 or Rs 6,000 the moment your shop grows. Ask what the price covers before you compare it with anything.

3. Your data is held hostage

Stop paying a monthly cloud plan and most vendors lock you out — of your own bills, your own khata balances, your own stock records. You have not bought software; you have rented your own business records, and the rent is due every month forever. If you stop paying, you lose access to the history of your own shop. Prefer software that keeps your data on your own computer, with your own daily backup, so no subscription can take it away.

4. Features behind paywalls

The base plan prints bills. Khata, barcode scanning, WhatsApp statements, expiry alerts, detailed reports — each one is an "add-on" at extra monthly cost. The plan you compared is not the plan you end up paying for. Add up the price of the plan with the features you actually need, then do the three-year maths on that number, not the headline.

5. Internet dependence

Most monthly plans are cloud software: no internet, no billing. During load shedding, a dead connection, or a slow mobile network, the counter stops — or you are back to the register book while still paying the monthly fee. The 3 Honest Costs of One-Time Payment

Fair is fair — one-time payment has real costs too, and a guide that hides them is a sales pitch, not a guide.

1. The cash leaves at once

Rs 40,000 in one day hurts more than Rs 1,500 a month, especially for a new shop that is still finding its feet. That is a genuine reason to hesitate. The honest answer is to try before you buy: run the software on your real counter for the full free trial — seven days of your real rush, your real items, your real customers — and judge the value after a week of actual use, not after a sales demo.

2. At the cheapest monthly tier, monthly can genuinely win

At Rs 500 a month, three years costs Rs 18,000 — less than half the one-time price. That is real, and we will not pretend otherwise. But check what Rs 500 actually buys: item limits, no khata, no barcode, no WhatsApp bills, and sometimes adverts on your own counter screen. Our guide to free billing software in Pakistan explains what cheap and free plans usually leave out — the same logic applies to the cheapest monthly tier.

3. Big upgrades may cost extra

Some one-time vendors charge for major new versions — you buy version 3, and version 4 is a new purchase. Ask before you buy: what is included for life — updates, support, new features? And what would ever cost extra? Get the answer in writing, not in a sales call. A one-time licence is only a good deal if "one-time" means what it says.

The 7 Questions to Ask Before You Choose

Run these seven questions on any software, monthly or one-time, including ours. A vendor who answers clearly is a vendor who has done this maths before. A vendor who changes the subject to the bill design has told you everything you need to know.

  1. What is the total I will pay in 3 years — the monthly fee × 36, including every add-on I need?
  2. Is the monthly price locked, or can it rise? By how much, and with how much notice?
  3. Does the headline price cover all my counters, users and branches — or is each one extra?
  4. If I stop paying, do I keep my data — every bill, every khata balance, every stock record — on my own computer?
  5. Which features cost extra each month (khata, barcode, WhatsApp bills, expiry alerts, reports)?
  6. Does it bill when the internet is down? What happens to my counter during load shedding?
  7. For a one-time licence: what exactly is included for life — updates, support, new features — and what would ever cost extra?

Who Should Choose Which?

Monthly is the right call if you are testing whether software suits your shop at all — pay for two or three months, learn the system, and then decide with experience instead of guesses. It can also be right if you run several branches that genuinely need cloud sync and you value that more than the cost, or if your cash flow honestly cannot take a one-time payment right now.

One-time is the right call if your shop is established and you plan to still be open in three years; if you want your data on your own PC where no subscription can lock it away; if you bill through load shedding and dead internet; or if you would rather pay once and stop thinking about it. For most settled Pakistani shops — kiryana, medical store, general store, wholesale — that is the list that matters.

And there is a middle path that costs nothing to try: use the free trial first. Seven days on your real counter, with your real rush, will tell you more than any pricing page. If the software survives your Monday, the one-time maths is almost always the cheaper road.

The Bottom Line

Monthly looks cheap because the number is small. Multiply it by 36 before you decide. At any monthly fee above roughly Rs 1,100, a Rs 40,000 one-time licence is cheaper within three years — and after that, the subscription keeps charging while the licence costs nothing more. The only honest exception is the very cheapest monthly tier, and even there, check what the cheap price leaves out before you celebrate it.

The right question was never "monthly or one-time?" It was always two questions: what do I pay over three years, and do I still own my data at the end of it? Answer those two, and the choice makes itself.
