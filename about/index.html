About shopcos soft

shopcos soft is smart shop management software built for small retailers — starting in Pakistan, now used by shops in 31 countries. We help kiryana stores, pharmacies, garment shops, mobile shops, and every kind of retail business run professionally — with fast barcode billing, stock tracking, khata/udhar management, and AI-powered insights.

Our Story

shopcos soft was created by COS — Chishti Office Samundri ("Online Work Solutions"), a technology company based in Samundri, Punjab, Pakistan. We saw small shopkeepers struggling with paper registers, lost udhar records, and expired stock — while expensive software was built for big chains, not for the common dukandaar.

So we built software designed for them: simple enough for any staff member to learn in a day, powerful enough to transform how a shop runs, and priced as a one-time payment — not a monthly burden. The software runs 100% offline on the shop's own computer, so billing never stops when the internet does.

Word spread beyond Pakistan. Today shopcos soft supports 28 currencies and 9 languages, and shopkeepers from South Asia to Africa to the Middle East use it to run their counters — but our home, and our heart, is still the small dukaan in Samundri.

Who We Build For

Independent retail shops of every kind: kiryana and general stores, medical stores and pharmacies (with batch and expiry tracking), cloth and garment shops, grocery stores, mobile and electronics shops, bakeries, hardware stores, and wholesalers. If you sell it from a counter, shopcos soft was made for you.

How We Work

Three principles guide everything we build. Your data is yours — shop data stays on your own computer, never held hostage on our servers. Simple beats fancy — if a shop's staff cannot use a feature without training, we redesign it. Honest pricing — one payment, yours forever; plans from $90 to $545 depending on shop size, with no subscriptions and no hidden charges.

Our Mission

To bring modern technology to every small shop — so no shopkeeper anywhere ever loses money to poor records again.
